Methodology

RSI and pivots

RSI uses Wilder's smoothing over 14 candle closes — the standard used by TradingView and most platforms. A pivot is an RSI low (or high) that is lower (or higher) than the 5 candles on each side. Price is compared at the same candles: the candle low for bullish setups, the candle high for bearish ones.

RSI = 100 − 100 / (1 + avg gain / avg loss) (Wilder, 14) Pivot = RSI extreme with 5 candles left and right Distance = 5 to 60 candles between the two pivots

A pivot needs 5 more candles to be confirmed, so every divergence appears 5 candles after the actual turning point — exactly like TradingView. Only completed candles are used, so signals never repaint.

Divergences marked oversold (RSI below 30) or overbought (RSI above 70) at the pivot are considered stronger. A divergence is a warning sign, not an entry signal on its own — combine it with structure, trend and risk management. 4h candles are built from 1h data starting at 17:00 New York time, matching the FX session used by TradingView.
