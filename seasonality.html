Methodology

Monthly statistics

Each month's return is the change from the previous month's close to that month's close. Statistics use the selected number of complete calendar years; the current, unfinished year is shown separately and never included in the averages.

Return(month) = Close(month) / Close(previous month) − 1 Average = mean of that month's returns across the years Bullish years = years in which the month closed higher Bearish years = years in which the month closed lower

Reading the numbers

The average shows how big the typical move was, the bullish / bearish count shows how consistent it was. A month is only a reliable pattern when both point the same way.

If the average is positive but most years were bearish (or the median is negative), a single strong year is driving the result — check the heatmap to see which one.

Seasonality describes historical tendencies, not forecasts. Central bank decisions, geopolitics and positioning can override any seasonal pattern. Small samples (e.g. 5 years) are easily driven by a single outlier year.
